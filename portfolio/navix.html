Case Study · Behavioral Health

Navix Health

A behavioral health operations platform — CRM, EHR, RCM, scheduling, documentation, and admin in one workspace. I led design across the system as it grew from a lightweight tool into a full operating platform for clinics.

Overview

Navix began as a lightweight system with a handful of basic modules — simple medication management, a client list, a hub, and document uploads. What it became is something much bigger: a behavioral health operations platform spanning CRM, EHR, revenue cycle management, scheduling, internal communications, and the admin surface that runs the business behind the clinic.

As Lead Designer, my work covered the entire workspace — not a single product area. That meant defining how clinical workflows (notes, incident reports, group sessions, rounding) coexisted with operational ones (billing, scheduling, time reporting, document management) without forcing clinicians and admins to learn two different products.

The EHR alone was a sub-platform of its own: 12+ top-level modules — Hub, Admissions, Clinical, Medical, Medications, Nursing, Labs, Documents, Outcomes, Attendance, Rounds — each containing nested workflows for notes, logs, assessments, and health metrics. The challenge wasn't just designing screens; it was designing reusable patterns that could behave consistently across dozens of different clinical contexts.

The Problem

Where Navix was falling short

The early Navix worked, but it didn't scale. Each module was being built in isolation — clinical tools, billing tools, scheduling tools — and the experience reflected that. Staff were forced to navigate around the product instead of through it, and the gaps showed up in every workflow.

✕ Before — Key challenges
  • Disconnected modules. CRM, clinical, and billing functions felt like separate products bolted together.
  • Minimal clinical depth. Documentation and medication tools were limited to add/edit/delete operations.
  • Generic documentation. Notes were static, without adaptation to encounter type or role.
  • Weak brand identity. Inconsistent UI gave the platform a "prototype" look across modules.
  • Workflow gaps. Clinicians lacked access to historical logs, outcomes, and context across the platform.
  • Inefficient space use. Data-heavy workflows crammed into layouts without hierarchy.
My Role

Lead designer across the whole platform

I led the design on Navix end-to-end, partnered with one junior designer, and worked directly with PMs, engineers, clinical advisors, and stakeholders. My responsibility wasn't a single product — it was the entire workspace: web and mobile, clinical and operational, internal-facing and admin.

  • Cross-module design — CRM, EHR (12+ clinical areas including Nursing, Clinical, Medications, Labs, Rounds, Group Notes, Incident Reports), RCM, Calendar, Time Reporting, Documents and Messaging
  • Reusable system patterns — Notes, Logs, Assessments, HealthMetrics, and Documents designed once and applied across multiple clinical modules
  • Admin surface — User Management, Form Templates, System Settings, Group Schedules, RCM Management, Reports, Integrations
  • Design system & governance — the foundation every new module was built on
  • Brand & UI refresh — refreshing visual identity for a more premium, enterprise-ready feel
  • Mentoring — guiding the junior designer through critiques, handoffs, and component decisions
  • Stakeholder collaboration — translating clinical and operational input into structured product decisions
The Approach

From scattered tools to one operating platform

The goal wasn't just to add features — it was to make Navix feel like one product instead of a collection of them. We expanded scope across CRM, EHR, RCM, scheduling, documentation, and admin tooling, while running a parallel branding and UI refresh to anchor the platform in a more premium identity. Each new module had to fit a shared design language and reinforce the system, not fragment it.

Learnings

What this work taught me

01

Brand is UX

The refreshed identity was as impactful as the new features in building trust. A clinical platform that looks unfinished feels unsafe — and clinicians notice.

02

One platform, not many products

Designing across CRM, EHR, RCM, scheduling, and admin in parallel taught me that cohesion is the feature. Behavioral health teams already juggle enough systems — Navix had to feel like one workspace, not 15 modules sharing a sidebar.

03

Clarity drives adoption

Structured notes and improved history views directly boosted clinician satisfaction. Adoption follows when the tool stops fighting the user.

04

Patterns over screens

In a platform this nested, designing individual screens doesn't scale. Logs, Assessments, and HealthMetrics had to behave the same way across Nursing, Medications, and Clinical — even though each held different data. The senior move was designing patterns, not pages.

05

Designing for density

In industries like healthcare, where information is vast and critical, smart use of space is as important as functionality. Every pixel pulls weight.

06

Cohesion is the feature

When users live inside a platform for hours a day, consistency stops being an aesthetic choice and becomes an operational one. A predictable system is a fast system.
